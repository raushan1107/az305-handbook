AZ-305 · Designing Microsoft Azure Infrastructure Solutions

Think like an architect, not like a service catalogue.

This handbook isn't a list of Azure services. It's about how a solution architect looks at a problem. Each module starts with a plain definition, explains it in simple words, gives you an analogy you won't forget, and then shows you the architect's lens: the questions to ask, the trade-offs to weigh and the decision you'd defend in front of a CTO or an exam question. Then a quick portal demo shows it for real.

Who this is for: Anyone preparing for AZ-305 or moving from developer or administrator into an architect role. No prior Azure experience is assumed — every term is introduced when it first appears. A single case study, Meridian Retail, runs through every module so you always see a decision in context, not in a vacuum.

Why this course feels different

AZ-104 asks "how do I build it?" AZ-305 asks "what should we build, and why?" Here's the shift.

01

Requirements first, services last

An architect never starts with "let's use AKS". They start with "what does the business actually need, and what can't it live without?"

02

Every choice is a trade-off

More availability costs money. More security adds friction. Your job is to choose the trade-off on purpose instead of falling into it.

03

Design for the bad day

Regions fail, people click the wrong button, credentials leak. An architecture is judged by how it behaves when things go wrong.

04

Keywords decide answers

"Minimise cost", "least administrative effort", "without code changes". In the exam and in real life, one phrase changes the right answer.
