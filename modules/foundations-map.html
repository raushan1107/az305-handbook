Foundations Map: Think, Govern, Secure, Observe

Part I is the part learners underestimate, because nothing in it runs an app. Yet every later design leans on it: the hierarchy decides where things live, policy decides what's allowed, identity decides who gets in, and monitoring decides whether anyone notices. This page puts all of it on one whiteboard.

    • Put numbers on reliableWords like "always up" become numbers3
      • SLA, SLO, SLIpromise, target, measurement

        The SLA is the provider's financially backed promise; the SLO is your own target; the SLI is what you actually measure.

        Use it whenAgreeing with the business what "available" means and how it's measured.
        Look elsewhere whenYou're asking how much data or time you can lose after a failure: that's RPO and RTO.
        Exam words99.9%uptimeservice creditavailability target
        Module 01 · SLA, SLO, RTO, RPO →
      • RTO and RPOhow long down, how much lost

        RTO: the maximum time to restore service. RPO: the maximum data loss, measured as time.

        Use it whenSizing backup, DR and replication; every continuity design starts here (Module 08).
        Look elsewhere whenThe question is about normal uptime, not recovery after a failure: use SLA and SLO.
        Exam wordsrecovery timedata lossminutes of data

        Meridian Tier 1 checkout RTO 15 min, RPO under 1 min; Tier 3 reporting RTO and RPO 24 h.

        Module 01 · SLA, SLO, RTO, RPO →
      • Composite SLAmultiply the chain

        The availability of services that depend on each other is the product of their SLAs; redundant paths raise it again.

        Use it whenChecking whether a chain of services can meet the target, and where redundancy is needed.
        Look elsewhere whenServices are independent of each other: their SLAs don't combine.
        Exam wordsmultiply SLAsdependenciesoverall availability
        Module 01 · Composite SLA →
    • Decide and recordMake the trade-off visible3

  • Organise and governWhere do resources live, and what rules apply?12
    • The hierarchyContainers inside containers3
      • Management groupsfolders of subscriptions

        Containers above subscriptions (up to six levels under the root) so policies and role assignments are inherited by every subscription below.

        Use it whenApplying the same guardrails to many subscriptions: Platform vs Landing zones vs Sandbox.
        Look elsewhere whenYou need to group resources for one app: use resource groups (or tags).
        Exam wordsmany subscriptionsinherit policyhierarchy

        Meridian Meridian → Platform (Identity, Management, Connectivity) / Landing zones (Corp, Online, PCI, Online-EU) / Sandbox / Decommissioned.

        Module 02 · Management groups →
      • Subscriptionsbilling, scale and isolation boundary

        A billing unit and a boundary for quotas, access and policy, trusting one Entra tenant.

        Use it whenSeparating business units, environments or compliance scopes (PCI) with their own bill and limits.
        Look elsewhere whenYou only need to group a few related resources: a resource group is enough.
        Exam wordsbilling boundaryisolationquota limits

        Meridian Per business unit and environment: sub-online-prod and friends.

        Module 02 · Subscriptions →
      • Resource groupsthings that live and die together

        A logical container for resources that share a lifecycle; every resource is in exactly one.

        Use it whenGrouping an app's resources so they're deployed, secured and deleted together.
        Look elsewhere whenResources have different owners or lifecycles: split them.
        Exam wordslifecycledelete togetherscope for RBAC
        Module 02 · Resource groups →

        Exam wordsenforcecomplianceallowed locationsrequire tag

        Meridian Allowed locations per landing zone; EU zone limited to Germany West Central.

Mindset takeaway

Most Part I questions are really "which control is this?" Policy decides what may exist; RBAC decides who may act; Conditional Access decides under which conditions they may sign in; monitoring decides whether you'd know. Name the control first, then the service.
